Research Interest

I work on post-training and reinforcement learning for agents that do long, expert work. A harness can make a model carry out long-horizon tasks today; I move that ability into the weights, building the whole pipeline: harnesses that produce training-grade trajectories, RL environments, evaluation benchmarks and reward design, then mid-training, SFT and RL. My long-term goal is agents that carry out arbitrarily complex, long-horizon expert work in digital environments, with a path toward real-world tasks; my current testbeds are agentic design and game development.

Evolvarium: the harness base for post-training
  • Designed and built Evolvarium (open source), the base the team's post-training iterates on across 3D modeling, slides, web and poster design, lifting LongCat 2.5's aesthetic and design ability substantially. Every run is sandboxed and recorded (inputs, harness revision, trace, grade) and exported as LongCat SFT data.
  • Meta-optimized harness (MoH): a Meta Agent edits instructions, skills, hooks and tools; a keep rule calibrated to measured noise keeps or reverts each edit. On our slides benchmark, the evolved harness lifts Kimi K3 from 57.6 to 74.7 (design +9.2, motion +9.5) and Qwen3.8-27B from 52.6 to 65.0, with no weight change.
  • Showed the grader is what the optimizer learns: a stricter grader cut every gain by a third to 70%; 8 of 9 harnesses kept after seeing failed rubric items copied it (0 of 11 with outcome-only feedback); write-up and 12 lineages.
Internalizing the harness: SFT, then RL
  • Goal: a student model in a plain harness (Claude Code, Pi) matching a teacher in the evolved one, with the harness's preferences, design principles and reusable scripts in its weights. Compared SFT recipes from a 20k synthesized slides pool: naive distillation, evolved-harness trajectories, offline turn-by-turn rewriting, online teacher intervention on student turns (to internalize tools and middleware), and runbook + hint distillation with leak audits.
  • Co-designed the tiered RL reward: validity, pixel-level usability, content against frozen per-query checklists, then a blind agentic aesthetics judge. Tier first, taste within tier, so a broken deck never outranks a usable one; the judge orders 59 of 60 calibration pairs. Fixed a GRPO zero-advantage failure by scoring broken tiers on bad pages.
