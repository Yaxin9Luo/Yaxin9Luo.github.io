# Moonlit garden art / 蓝色月夜花园

The current scene uses a blue moonlit sky, a detailed lunar sphere, lavender and pink flowering trees, silver-blue foliage, meadow flowers and warm paper lanterns. The visible sky is **not the earlier daylight HDR panorama**. The CC0 `environment/night.hdr` remains a low-strength lighting source. Existing PBR asset provenance is in [world-asset-sources.md](world-asset-sources.md).

## Delivered textures and actual use

The three files total **1,343,734 bytes / 1.28 MiB**, separate from the 37 CC0 material/HDR files. Exact hashes, dimensions and source fields are in `world/public/art/night-garden/manifest.json`. All three returned HTTP 200 through Vite, and response SHA-256 matched the files on disk.

| `/art/night-garden/blossom-atlas.webp` | Built-in ImageGen; generated for this project, 1254 × 1254 RGBA. | `landscape.js` crops top-left cherry blossoms, top-right lilac sprays and bottom-left bellflower stems. These are alpha-tested tree and meadow cards, not reference images left unused. |
| `/art/night-garden/moon-lroc-2k.jpg` | Unmodified NASA LRO color map, 2048 × 1024. | `atmosphere.js` maps it to the lunar sphere. A restrained unlit shader retains maria and crater detail at night. |

Generated PNGs were converted to WebP quality 95, method 6 with Pillow 12.3.0. There was no repainting or AI image-to-3D conversion. Alpha is present from 0 to 255 with transparent space between botanical silhouettes. The delivered dimensions above are actual tool output, not the larger dimensions requested in the prompt. ImageGen output is documented as generated artwork, **not CC0**.

The moon source is NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's [CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720), specifically its [2K LROC color map](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_2k.jpg). Credit: **NASA Scientific Visualization Studio / Ernie Wright / NASA-GSFC / LROC**. The [SVS usage statement](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/) and [NASA images and media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) govern this imagery. It is not represented as a CC0 asset, and no NASA logo or endorsement is used. No proprietary game material was extracted.

## Generation record

The first sky used the user-provided moonlit Gothic castle image as a color reference. Full-scene review then found excessive bright cloud coverage. The delivered second sky uses the ImageGen atmosphere target made from that actual scene screenshot as a sky-only reference. Buildings, UI, terrain, lanterns and the moon were expressly excluded; the moon remains independent 3D geometry at the original position. The first sky hash and generation filename remain in the manifest history. The flower atlas used no reference image.

Final sky prompt, built-in ImageGen, 2026-09-08 local date:

> Use case: photorealistic-natural. Asset type: actual 2:1 panoramic SKY TEXTURE for the inside of a 3D game sky dome, at least 2048x1024. Input image is a SKY ATMOSPHERE REFERENCE ONLY. Primary request: create only the deep cobalt-blue night sky visible in that reference, without any part of the scene or UI. This is a clear moonlit night, NOT dusk, NOT daylight, NOT an overcast sky. About 85 percent of the sky should be beautiful open, calm ink-blue / deep sapphire space, with small pin-sharp silver stars varying naturally in brightness and size. Tiny realistic stars, no giant bokeh, no fantastical galaxy or aurora. Only a few very thin, long, delicate, transparent silver-blue cirrus wisps and small broken low cloud strips, mainly in the lower third. Their contrast must stay low, never white or gray, and large areas must be completely cloud-free. Upper two thirds mostly pristine blue star field with subtle natural atmospheric gradients. For sky-dome mapping: horizontally continuous 360-degree panorama with matching left/right edges. Put the lowest cloud horizon around 75 percent down the image, then smoothly fade the bottom quarter into uniform dark navy; the upper pole area should be calm. Rich but restrained blue nighttime colors, light moonlit cloud edges, high-end realistic fantasy RPG atmosphere. NO MOON and NO SUN in this texture: a separate 3D moon already occupies the correct place. Absolutely NO castle, landscape, mountains, water, ground, horizon silhouettes, vegetation, buildings, people, lanterns, text, logo, interface, frame or border. Deliver only the seamless sky texture itself edge to edge, not a picture of a sphere, scene, mockup or screen. Do not reproduce any layout or text from the reference image.

Create ONE square 1024x1024 image on a genuinely transparent background with 4 separate botanical specimens arranged in an exact 2x2 grid, each specimen completely inside its own square cell, with generous transparent padding and NO dividing lines. ## Scene integration

`loadLandscapeAssets()` preloads the art through `loadAtmosphereAssets()`. The module is safe to import in Node without a DOM. Failed individual art downloads retain the authored blue sky and plain moon fallback rather than rejecting the whole scene preload.

`createAtmosphere(scene, { heightAt })` returns `update(t, dt, reduced)`, `releaseLantern(position)`, and `dispose()`. Its default background group contains 26 lanterns; manual release uses a fixed pool of 8. Each lantern has four meshes: paper shell, merged bamboo frame, flame and soft circular glow. Fine paper weave and stitched folds are shader detail. There is no point light per lantern. Manual lights rise slowly, expire, and stop moving with the reduced-motion setting. Invalid release coordinates are rejected. Releasing more than eight reuses the oldest slot.

`createTreeSpecimen('lilac' | 'cherry' | 'silver' | 'pine')` and `createSkyLantern()` supply independent studio specimens. Main terrain placement uses the current `islands`, landmark radii and court coordinates. Tree count is capped at 112, approximately 48% below the previous 215 on the expanded layout; half are low ornamental flowering trees. Placement keeps landmark footprints, paths and a broad front approach clear. Silver-leaf tint preserves botanical texture variation. The distant blue highland rings are lower around the moon bearing so that the castle silhouette has sky behind it.

The moon direction starts at normalized `(-0.16, 0.18, -0.84)`. The water highlight uses the same direction. The sky shader is unlit and independent of the camera's exposure; its cloud texture is multiplied by 0.68 in linear space. Stars use 1,800 fixed 1.1 px points without distance attenuation or fog so that they remain visible at the 870 m sky radius. The cloud shader samples neighboring low-mip texels to suppress enlarged star blobs in the generated bitmap; native point geometry supplies the crisp stars. The lunar sphere is rotated toward the near-side maria visible in the NASA image; its world-space location does not change. The integrated game owns its final hemisphere/key/fill settings; the local specimen review used ACES exposure 1.2, a blue hemisphere of 2.2, moon key of 3.4 and soft lilac fill of 1.0. These are artistic calibration values, not measured physical illuminance.

## Verification evidence

- Independent real-browser garden, lantern close-up and moon/sky specimens were rendered at `127.0.0.1:4190`; no shader compile errors occurred. The moon initially clipped to white; the reviewed shader and orientation were adjusted to reveal actual lunar maria. The second sky was also re-rendered, with its excess bitmap star blobs suppressed and horizon color darkened after local inspection. Silver leaf tint and distant ridge heights were revised after rendering.
- The temporary specimen HTML was used only for local QA and moved into ignored `work/night-garden/`. Screenshots are `garden-detail.png`, `lantern-detail.png`, and `moon-sky-detail.png` in that directory. Its direct Three.js imports produced a duplicate-library warning unique to that temporary preview; the production modules use the existing project imports.
- The existing terrain and collision suites passed: 15 tests. A Node smoke checked DOM-free imports, finite lantern geometry, 4 meshes per lantern, 8-slot reuse, invalid-input rejection, manual-light expiration, reduced-motion positional stability and disposal.
- The asset audit confirmed all three delivered files, dimensions, flower alpha and HTTP response hashes. This is evidence for these assets and their local specimens; the complete game is evaluated separately by the main scene QA.
