# Character assets / 角色资产

Two Blender assets with original geometry replace the first geometric character sketches: a broom-riding researcher and a spectral archive guardian. They use an original fantasy visual vocabulary and are not likenesses of film characters or a 3D portrait of Yaxin Luo. The researcher is intentionally stylized; this is not a scanned or photoreal human.

## Files and provenance

| Asset | Geometry | Embedded textures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `world/public/models/characters/wizard.glb` | Original geometry generated by `scripts/art/build-characters.py` | Real Poly Haven cloth/leather albedo, normal and roughness maps; original mathematical walnut/birch grain patterns |
| `world/public/models/characters/wraith.glb` | Same original script | Real Poly Haven wool maps, brass and emissive eyes |

The two external material sources are [Poly Wool Herringbone](https://polyhaven.com/a/poly_wool_herringbone), photographed by colormass and processed by Rico Cilliers, and [Brown Leather](https://polyhaven.com/a/brown_leather), by Rob Tuytel. Both are published under [Poly Haven's CC0 asset license](https://polyhaven.com/license). No downloaded character mesh is used.

Source copies live in `world/public/textures/wool-cloth/` and `world/public/textures/dark-leather/`, each containing `color.webp`, `normal.webp` and `roughness.webp`. The texture team's `world/public/textures/manifest.json` records acquisition provenance and checksums. Source maps are 1K, with OpenGL normal orientation. Wood patterns are deterministic script-generated pixels. No image-generation output is represented as a real asset photograph.

Cloth and leather use real albedo, tangent normals and roughness in both Blender and the exported GLB. Their tints and mapping scales survive export through `KHR_texture_transform`; embedded WebP images use `EXT_texture_webp`. Cloth mapping uses approximately 3.7 tiles per metre and leather 2.5 where panel dimensions are explicitly mapped. The shader does not use a flat roughness value instead of the scanned roughness map.

Final assets contain 45,059 triangles / 3,643,736 bytes for the rider and 12,998 triangles / 1,523,468 bytes for the guardian (5,167,204 bytes together). The authoritative triangle, mesh and byte counts are in `world/public/models/characters/manifest.json`. Static pieces are combined by material, and geometry is simplified after subdivision. Six guardians share the same buffers, materials and textures through Three.js scene cloning.

## Design and studio review

The researcher has a shaped shoulder/waist silhouette, folded coat lapels, a waistcoat, visible buttons and pockets, split lined coat tails, boot soles and side seams, curved fingers grasping the broom, facial features and short swept hair. The burgundy scarf has ochre bands and fringe. The broom has a curved wood handle, leather grip winding, metal ferrules and 72 individually shaped twig strands.

The guardian has a recessed pointed hood, narrow spectral eyes, a three-layer robe with uneven hems, shaped sleeves, thin fingers and a brass talisman. The silhouette remains readable without bloom or game particles.

Earlier Cycles iterations were inspected from front and back. The latest shoulder, lapel and drapery correction used actual 10-sample previews at 750 × 825 before GLB export, followed by 40-sample final front renders at 1000 × 1100. Lighting remains a warm key, cooler fill and rim light. The retained back PNGs document the earlier iteration, not this final correction. Review found and corrected:

1. Hair strands originally floated above the skull and read as thick parallel rails. They now follow a fitted scalp surface with much finer irregular strands. The head is 23% shorter and 14% narrower, with a modeled nose and sewn shirt collar.
2. The original hood read as a round head. Its opening is now built from eight angular cloth panels, with a deep interior. Longer sleeves conceal most of the hands and only short tapered claws remain visible.
3. The original dark interior face protruded through the narrowing hood and produced a black arc from the back. It is now a recessed polygon mask inside the opening; the hood also closes to a single connected terminal pole.
4. Scarf stripes originally looked like floating rods. They are now flat material bands assigned directly to the scarf surface; fringe shares the animated hierarchy.
5. Overlong arms and identical accordion coat tails were corrected. The grip is raised, the arms shortened, and the cloth has broad asymmetric folds and sewn edges.
6. Smooth placeholder cloth/leather was replaced with real scanned PBR maps and physical panel UVs.
7. Ball-like shoulder caps and uniform upper sleeves were narrowed and replaced by tapered shoulder/elbow/forearm sections, with seams placed on the evaluated surface.
8. Crossed chest panels were replaced by conforming gridded lapels and waistcoat; buttons and pockets now sit on the actual coat surface.
9. The guardian sleeves are thinner and asymmetric. The shoulder layer follows the inner robe with a real clearance, removing interpenetration and replacing the scalloped paper-like hem with continuous drape.
10. Original geometry exceeded the budgets. Applied subdivision and simplification retain folds and the visible silhouette at the final counts in the manifest.

Reviewed render paths, relative to the surrounding `outputs` directory:

- `qa/wizard-studio-back.png` (prior iteration)
- `qa/wraith-studio-front.png`

The close-up face and garment folds remain stylized. This is still a custom game character, not a production-scanned actor asset. These studio checks establish geometry and material appearance in Blender, not the final lighting, camera scale or frame rate in the website. The integrated Three.js scene still requires its own visual and performance review.

## Runtime contract

```js
import { loadCharacterAssets, createWizard, createWisp } from './characters.js';
await loadCharacterAssets();
const wizard = createWizard();
const guardian = createWisp();
```

`loadCharacterAssets({baseURL})` is optional-argument, asynchronous and deduplicates concurrent requests. Default URL is `/models/characters/`. It rejects asset loading or missing-anchor errors so the application can surface them. A failed request can be retried.

The synchronous factories return independent Three.js object hierarchies sharing geometry, material and texture storage. Before loading, they return the existing procedural models for graceful fallback and Node simulation compatibility. Importing the module does not touch `window`, `document`, a WebGL context or the network.

The wizard faces local −Z and preserves these object references:

| Property | GLB node | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `userData.cape` | `rider-cape` | Group rotation around shoulder attachment; `restRotationX` records the base pose |
| `userData.scarf` | `rider-scarf` | Rotation around neck attachment, with stripes/fringe moving together |
| `userData.wandTip` | `wandTip` | Spell world-position anchor |
| `userData.broomTail` | `broomTail` | Trail world-position anchor |

There is no skeleton or skeletal animation. The current flight pose is modeled into the meshes; cloth receives the existing small procedural rotations. The wraith's movement is controlled by the existing world/group animation.

## Rebuild / 重建

From the repository root, on a Mac with Blender installed:

```sh
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender \
  --background --factory-startup \
  --python scripts/art/build-characters.py
```

Verified with Blender 5.1.2. The command expects the two CC0 texture sets above to be present. It starts an isolated factory scene, builds both GLBs and their two final front studio renders, and writes the manifest. It never connects to or modifies a user's open Blender scene. Append `-- --preview` for a 10-sample front review without overwriting the GLBs or manifest.

Node syntax checking and a direct Node import confirmed both synchronous fallback factories return groups and all four wizard animation anchors are available without browser globals. This is an import/contract check, not a WebGL rendering test.

Final validation also parsed both GLB containers, checked triangle counts against the manifest, confirmed all four rider anchors and exported normal/roughness maps, and received HTTP 200 for both assets from the local Vite preview. These are file/availability checks; the parent task owns the integrated browser render check.
