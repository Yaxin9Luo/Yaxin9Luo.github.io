# Material restoration after the scene review

The reviewed v2 scene had retained the source texture downloads while suppressing most of their visual effect. Broad walls used a normal strength of `0.055` and a colour-map influence of `0.12`; roofs used `0.045` and `0.09`. The lawn used `0.06` and `0.18`. Masonry, roofing and lawn roughness maps had also been removed. This made real scans read as flat painted surfaces.

The correction restores source detail to the broad surfaces and keeps separately modelled trim quieter. It changes material bindings and colours, not the geometry, lights, resolution, foliage, lake or terrain placement.

The roughness interval is sampled through the real roughness image. The lower bound prevents a dark roughness texel from turning limestone or slate into polished plastic. Roughness is not flattened to a constant. Slate is explicitly a dielectric; copper retains its independent metalness map. Source textures still use shared allocations, repeat wrapping, mipmaps and eight-fold anisotropy through the existing loader.

The 15 m meadow texture retains its measured scale. The terrain's rock overlay now samples the 3 m rock scan at `terrainPosition.xz / 3`, instead of stretching it across roughly 26 m. Broad grass/rock transitions combine the scanned meadow, slope and larger weathering patches. No source image was rescaled or replaced.

Materials that explicitly request `map: null` and `normalMap: null`, such as some carved garden trim, keep that choice. Thin courses and mouldings already contain geometric relief and do not inherit the wall-strength normal setting.

## Source and validation

The existing self-hosted CC0 inputs and their checksums remain unchanged in `world/public/textures/manifest.json`: Poly Haven Old Stone Wall 02, Roof Slates 02, Mossy Rock, Aerial Grass Rock, Weathered Planks, and ambientCG Metal 058 C. This pass introduces no additional texture files or downloads.

`node --test world/tests/materials.test.js` passed all three tests. They exercise the real shared loader and material-binding path, substituting image decoding in Node. They verify available local source files, colour spaces, shared texture identity, differentiated broad/trim detail, sampled roughness, shader hook composition, measured rock scale and preservation of explicitly plain materials. These are binding regressions; they do not substitute for the root task's live WebGL screenshots and interaction tests.

The historic architecture GLB inspection renders are not regenerated by this pass. The current website uses `models.js` geometry and the restored runtime bindings; a historic Blender PNG should not be presented as a screenshot of this change.

## Close-range garden props

The same correction now covers the real reading desks, shelves, books, correspondence folder, fountain metalwork and planters created by `gardens.js`. Their material assignments are tested by constructing the reading-garden geometry and traversing the actual materials.

- Walnut tabletops and structural timber use the existing Weathered Planks colour/normal/roughness set at a 2 m repeat. Edge timber has a gentler normal response than broad boards.
- Planter bodies keep visible masonry; carved coping retains fine surface relief without inheriting wall-strength stone courses. The paving has lower relief appropriate to a honed walking surface, and the foundation uses the scanned mossy rock.
- Instrument rings and small metal feet use the existing copper oxidation colour, normal and roughness maps, with a restrained 0.44 colour influence so the authored brass still reads warm. These props use uniform metalness 0.72; unlike the building copper, they do not use a separate metalness image.
- Blue and wine book covers use the existing herringbone wool scan at approximately 27.3 cm per repeat. Paper and readable lettering remain distinct materials.
- Planter soil uses the existing 2.14 m forest-floor scan, including grain and fallen leaves, instead of a uniform dark swatch.

The garden builder's 2.8 m UV basis is unchanged. Material-local UV scaling aligns colour, normal and roughness channels at each source's measured scale without changing the shared texture object or rebuilding geometry. The copper scale is an authored 1 m repeat because its provider gives no usable physical dimension. The landscape preload now asks the existing cache for wood, copper and cloth channels; existing building/character users continue sharing the decoded texture sources where they use the same loader.

The fourth material regression test passed together with the preceding three. It checks the actual prop assignments, shared wood texture identity, map-channel alignment, sub-metre fabric repeat, soil detail and the separation of carved trim, broad stone and metal roughness. Browser close-ups are still required to judge their final appearance under the scene's lighting. Plant geometry and its wind shader are outside this material patch.
