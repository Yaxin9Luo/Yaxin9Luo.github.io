STYLE REFERENCE: the attached picture is a reference for the PALETTE AND HAND ONLY (dull mineral azurite and malachite over warm ochre, a fine ink outline, short texture strokes, the matt faded look of an old silk handscroll). Match that palette and hand; draw the new subject described below, not the mountains in the reference. Use the azurite and malachite boldly where the subject allows (roof undersides, pines, rocks, moss, cloth), and keep a firm dark ink outline so the piece reads small. Viewpoint: from about 35 degrees above the ground, the piece facing the viewer.
SUBJECT: the palace city of Zhaoge (朝歌), the last Shang capital, rising behind its city wall (the wall itself is NOT drawn: the bottom of the picture is a straight horizontal cut across the bases of the buildings). On the left the Star-Plucking Tower (摘星楼): an enormously tall, slender tower of many storeys of dark timber and galleries on a high rammed-earth base, so tall its top is among the clouds. On the right the Deer Terrace (鹿台): a vast stepped terrace of rammed earth faced with white stone and jade-green tiles, long stairways up its front, splendid halls on its summit. Between them palace halls with double hipped thatch roofs and red-lacquered posts. Very many tall black and white Shang banners and long streamers on poles, bronze-topped. Over the tower top and the terrace hangs a dark, coiling, ominous cloud of ink-black and dull purple with a faint red demonic glow inside it (妖气). Splendid, dark and doomed. FRAMING: the whole palace city fills the width of the picture, buildings reaching up to the top third; the clouds fill the top.
