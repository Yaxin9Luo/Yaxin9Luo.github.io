A single isolated piece of scenery for a 2D game, painted in the manner of a Chinese blue-green landscape handscroll (青绿山水) with ruled-line architecture (界画), in the hand of a Song court painter.
MEDIUM: ink and mineral colour on silk. A fine, even, confident ink outline round every form; mineral colours laid flat and slightly mottled in large areas: azurite blue (石青), malachite green (石绿), red-brown ochre (赭石), pale lead white; rock and earth textured with short ink texture strokes (皴) and moss dots (苔点). Dull, antique, matt pigments, never bright or saturated. No gradients of light, no cast shadows, no highlights, no 3D rendering, no glow.
VIEW: seen from a little above at a slant, the way a handscroll shows a building or a hill (an oblique bird's-eye view, roofs and terraces visible from above but walls still visible), one consistent viewpoint, front facing the viewer.
GROUND: the piece stands on a small patch of its own ground that fades out softly to nothing at its edges. Nothing else: no horizon, no sky, no distant scenery.
BACKGROUND: completely plain transparent background: no paper texture, no frame, no border, no text, no seal, no signature, no people, no animals.
LEGIBILITY: a bold clear silhouette that reads when the piece is shown small (about 300 to 500 pixels tall); large simple shapes; ornament kept large.
AVOID: photorealism, Western painting, cartoon or anime style, Japanese temple style, curved upturned eaves of later dynasties (this is the Shang–Zhou age, about 1050 BCE: rammed-earth platforms, hipped thatch roofs, timber posts, bronze vessels), modern details.
